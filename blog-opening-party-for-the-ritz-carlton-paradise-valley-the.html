Opening Party for
The Ritz-Carlton
Paradise Valley The Palmeraie

Paradise Valley has a new landmark, and on opening night the corridor between the resort and the runway felt busier than it has in years. A few notes from the evening, written the next morning.

Filed under
Neighborhood
Where
Paradise Valley, AZ
Read time
4 minutes

An evening out, seen through the eyes of a kitchen and a dining room that work a few minutes north of it.

The Ritz-Carlton Paradise Valley opened its doors with a party, and the party is now part of the larger project called The Palmeraie. It sits a short drive south of Scottsdale Airport, close enough that most of our regulars will pass it on the way to dinner.

Palms line the drive in long rows. Water runs through the courtyards, the buildings stay low, and everything is lit warm and from below, which is the kind of lighting that flatters a crowd at dusk. By the time the sun dropped behind Camelback, the whole property had turned gold.

The room at eleven o'clock

Anyone can set a beautiful table at six. The real test of a new property comes later in the night, when the first wave has eaten, the second wave has arrived, and the staff has been on its feet for hours. Trays kept moving. Glasses kept getting cleared. Nobody was left standing at a door wondering where to go next.

That is the part worth stealing. Service reads as hospitality when the pacing is right and the floor is read correctly, and no amount of architecture covers for a slow pour.

What it means for this corner of the Valley

A resort of this size changes the traffic around it. More visitors head north on Scottsdale Road, more of them are looking for somewhere to eat that is not inside their hotel, and more of them have an afternoon to fill between arriving and dinner. That is good news for every kitchen in the area, ours included.

It also raises the bar, which we take as a compliment to the neighborhood. Guests arriving from a property like that one carry a standard with them. They notice how fast the water gets poured and whether the host remembers their name on the way out.

Back on the runway

Our own view is a different one. We look out at taxiing aircraft instead of palm courtyards, and the show outside the window changes every few minutes. Different setting, same job: get the timing right, keep the glasses full, send people home talking about the evening rather than the wait.

Congratulations to everyone who put that night together. It was a long build and a good opening.
